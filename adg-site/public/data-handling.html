Your data, handled safely.
And proven, every step.

The process, step by step

1

We agree what's included, and start small

We start from the agreement. First we write down exactly what's included: which systems, which record types, which dates, and anything you want left out. Then you send us a small sample to look at, not your whole dataset, so we only move what we need. Anything that does move is sent encrypted, lands in a locked spot only our named team can open, and every time someone opens it, it's logged.

2

We write a plan, and you approve it

We look at the sample and find where any personal information is: names, contacts, dates, notes, and so on. Then we write a simple plan that says, column by column, what we'll remove, hide, or blur. You read it and approve it before we touch the full dataset. Nothing is shown to buyers at this stage.

3

We remove the personal details

Now we clean the data where it lives, using our own tools, and only the cleaned copy ever leaves. We take out or hide names, contacts, and ID numbers; we blur exact dates and locations; and we scrub personal details out of any free-text notes. If records need to stay linked, we swap the IDs for codes that can't be traced back, so the data still connects without pointing to anyone.

An honest note: removing personal details lowers the chance someone could be identified again, but no method can promise that is impossible. For sensitive data such as health records, a qualified expert reviews and signs off first.

4

A person double-checks it

The software is the first pass, not the last word. A real person reviews a batch of the cleaned data to make sure nothing personal slipped through, paying special attention to free-text notes, and looks for unusual combinations (like a rare job title in a small town) that could still point to one person. Two people, one technical and one compliance, have to sign off before any buyer sees it.

5

Buyers work in a locked room, not with a copy

Your prepared data sits in a clean room: a locked, monitored workspace. A buyer can work with the data inside that room and see results, but they can't walk away with a raw copy of everything. Only named people get in, access is time limited, and every entry is logged. If data is ever delivered, it's done the way your agreement spells out.

6

You get a full record of what we did

Every step is written down. You get a report that shows what was included, a digital fingerprint of your original file, exactly what we did to each column, how many things we scrubbed from notes, who opened the data and when, the sign-offs, and confirmation of what we did not keep. It's your proof that your data was handled exactly as agreed.

7

We keep as little as possible, then delete it

We don't keep your original, identifiable data, and we don't keep any key that could trace the cleaned data back to people. We delete the detailed bits within the time your agreement sets, and we either return your source data or send you a certificate proving we deleted it. If anyone ever makes a legal request for your data, we tell you where we're allowed and share only what the law requires.

8

You stay in charge

You own your data the whole time and approve every buyer and every use. Every buyer has to agree, in writing, not to identify anyone again and not to pass the data on. Nothing happens outside what you approved.

Why this beats a typical broker

A typical broker introduces a buyer and then hands your data to some other company to prepare it. They can't tell you who touched it or what became of it. We keep the whole thing in house, one team start to finish, and we write it all down. We can show you what we did to every record, prove what we removed, and confirm what we did not keep. A broker that only makes introductions can't give you that.
